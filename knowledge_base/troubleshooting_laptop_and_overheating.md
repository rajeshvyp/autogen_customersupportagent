# Troubleshooting Guide — Laptops and Overheating
Version: 1.0 | Owner: Technical Service

## Laptop will not power on
1. Check that the power adapter is connected to a working outlet.
2. Inspect the cable and adapter for visible damage.
3. Disconnect non-essential peripherals.
4. If there are no safety warning signs, follow the model's documented power-reset procedure.
5. If the device remains unresponsive, arrange Technical Service assessment.

## Overheating
Possible causes include blocked vents, high workload, unsuitable surfaces or a hardware fault. Ask when the issue occurs and whether the fan is unusually loud.

Safe guidance:
- Place the laptop on a hard, flat surface.
- Keep ventilation openings unobstructed.
- Save work and shut down if temperature warnings appear.
- Do not open the chassis or remove the battery unless the official service instructions specifically permit it.

If there is smoke, burning smell, swelling, sparks or excessive heat, stop using the device and escalate immediately. Do not continue troubleshooting.

## Slow performance
Ask whether the issue affects all applications, whether storage is nearly full and whether updates are pending. Avoid recommending destructive resets before data backup.

## Battery drains quickly
Ask about age, workload, screen brightness and connected peripherals. Battery capacity naturally changes with use; a sudden change requires assessment.

## Evidence to collect
Model, serial number if available, operating system, error message, time of occurrence and steps already tried.
